2022 — Early 2023

Chatbots

Knows but doesn't do

The ChatGPT moment blew minds. Ask a question from a web browser, get an answer. But in a very short space of time, context windows ran out and you kind of ran out of things to do with it. Useful on the side, but limited.

2023 — Early 2025

Reasoning

Plans multi-step tasks

Chain-of-thought prompting changed the game. Models began planning in multiple steps, and tools like LangChain agents emerged. Feeding context back into the system produced progressively better outcomes — each loop smarter than the last.

Mid 2023 — 2024

Tool Use

Can call tools and APIs

The first big shift. Models gained access to the outside world — browsing the internet, connecting to APIs, Zapier, Wolfram Alpha. Remember when "book me a flight" was the killer demo? Not a great use case, but it proved capability.

2025

Operator

Browser actions on your behalf

ChatGPT got its own browser. Moving a mouse, clicking buttons, interpreting screenshots. It wasn't just answering — it was acting. Slow, yes. But the shift from responding to doing was profound. Not just knowing, but operating.

Late 2025 — Early 2026

Coworker

Human still in the loop

Claude Code. AI moved directly onto your device — manipulating files, using browsers, writing code in your workspace. The paradigm shift: AI coming closer to you. But every action still requires a human click. "I accept that this is what's happening."

2026

Autonomous

Always on. Event-triggered.

Dedicated hardware. Full permissions — emails, calendar, browser. People talk to agents via Telegram and WhatsApp while the agent works continuously in the background. Mac Minis sold out. It's always on, acting on triggers even when you're not asking.

3 Bucket Challenge

a future proof exercise

01
Bucket One 01 —

Tasks AI Can Do
80% as Well

Routine tasks that are roughly 80% automatable — let AI carry the weight here.

Examples
  • Basic research and info gathering
  • Reformatting content across formats
  • Editing content drafts
  • Summarizing long documents or reports into key takeaways
  • Building comparison tables or pros/cons lists from research
  • Creating agendas from scattered prep notes
  • Drafting routine emails
02
Bucket Two 02 —

Taste, Judgement &
Strategic Vision

High-level work involving strategy, taste, and decision-making — where human discernment matters most.

Examples
  • Curating info for specific audiences
  • Deciding which projects to prioritize and why
  • Knowing when 'good enough' is sufficient
  • Reading the room in a meeting and adjusting your approach on the fly
  • Recognizing when a process is broken
  • Knowing when to push back on a request vs. just get it done
  • Understanding the unwritten politics around a decision
03
Bucket Three 03 —

Personality or
Lived Experience

Personality or lived experience fueled by your unique background and character — irreplaceable and distinctly human.

Examples
  • Building trust through authenticity
  • The questions you ask that others don't think to ask
  • Your vibe
  • Mentoring someone through a career decision
  • Your instinct for when humor will land vs. when it won't
  • The connections you draw between unrelated ideas
  • Knowing which hill to die on and which to let go

Step 2

Optimize for Buckets 2 & 3,
learn to orchestrate Bucket 1
